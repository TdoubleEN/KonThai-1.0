# Selective language guidance

Use only the section relevant to the actual problem. These are editing signals, not bans or proof of AI authorship.

- Repeated lead-ins: “นอกจากนี้ / อีกทั้ง / ยิ่งไปกว่านั้น” may become a connected sentence. Keep connectors that express a real cause, exception or sequence.
- Translation-like syntax: simplify “ในการที่จะ / มีความสามารถในการ / ถูกออกแบบมาเพื่อ” when the actor and meaning stay intact. Passive voice can be correct when the actor is unknown or unimportant.
- Empty importance: remove “ในยุคปัจจุบัน / อย่างแท้จริง / ถือเป็นก้าวสำคัญ” when no information is lost. Do not invent measurements, taste, texture, release dates or customer experience to replace vague promotional wording. Unsupported claims need a separate note or source check.
- Genuine hooks: preserve a meaningful “ทำไม...” question when the piece answers it. Avoid replacing an approved reason-led story with a chronological event recap.
- Repetition: merge repeated ideas, but keep deliberate repetition, exact technical terms and all distinct facts. A real list of three facts stays three. Do not use synonyms for a product component just to vary wording.
- Thai register: preserve the writer's ครับ/ค่ะ/นะ choices where appropriate. Business prose stays respectful; concise does not require slang. Do not insert a private nickname or conversational form of address into audience-facing copy.
- Spoken copy: read for breath and articulation; retain actual procedural order and warnings. Introduce an unfamiliar technical term only if the audience needs it.
- Executive copy: put the decision or finding early, then evidence, tradeoffs and unresolved constraints. Do not compress away why the recommendation follows.
- English and mixed text: use plain, idiomatic syntax; keep product names and established English technical terms. Preserve meaningful hedges, quotes, typography and reader-appropriate formality. No forced contractions, fragments or artificial errors.

When a user requests proofreading only, preserve headings, paragraph order and creative framing. When the user supplies a voice sample, follow it over generic style habits. Do not infer a permanent rule from one approved caption or a fixed campaign post count.
